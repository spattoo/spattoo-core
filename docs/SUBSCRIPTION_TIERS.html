Spark is a 30-day free TRIAL — a CARD on the pricing page, never a COLUMN.

Spark does appear on the pricing page, as one of three cards (Spark · Flame · Blaze). What it must never be is a column in the comparison matrix. The original failure was not the label — it was the tick-by-tick comparison: readers ran their eye across the rows, saw ✓/✓/✓/✓ on designer, storefront, branding and order management, and concluded Flame added nothing. Calling the column “Trial” does not fix that; the ticks still line up. So Spark’s card body is messaging, not a feature list — “Everything unlocked. Free for 30 days. No card needed. After 30 days, pick a plan.” Any detailed comparison table below has columns Flame | Blaze only. (In THIS engineering doc Spark stays a greyed column — here we need to see what it grants. The rule is about the customer-facing page.) Three cards also means the Forge decision can wait: the page does not look thin without it.

⚠️ Pending sign-off — what does the trial grant? The card copy only works if the trial equals ONE tier. Today Spark is a hybrid (same as Flame on most rows, but craft guides preview vs full, templates 3 vs 30, smart tools trial vs small) — unwritable as a one-liner. Recommended: trial = BLAZE for 30 days (“Start with everything”) — the textbook reverse trial, 24% median conversion vs 14% for a plain opt-in trial, and it aims people at THE TARGET tier. Alternative: trial = Flame (safer, weaker). Accept the tradeoff: trialling Blaze then buying Flame feels like a downgrade — that is loss aversion working as designed, but pre-empt it in the trial-ending email. Changes seed_plan_entitlements.sql, so decide before shipping.

Every plan includes — the category features

  • Full 3D cake designer
  • Every decoration, finish, piping & topper
  • A4 edible-print simulator
  • Order & quote management
  • Public storefront + your branding
  • Colour guide
  • Cake flavours catalog
  • Email notifications

These are never gated. Their job is to justify the price LEVEL — why the floor is ₹999 and not ₹299, and why a generic store builder (Shopify, Instamojo, Dukaan) is not a comparable product. Gating a category feature destroys that argument, which is why element-tier-gating was killed. But they must be visible: the A4 simulator defended nothing while it was buried inside “photos, PDF”.

Upgrade levers

Trial → Flame: Flame is NOT sold against Spark — it is sold against a notebook, a phone gallery and Instagram DMs. The story: “I pay ₹999 so my customers can see my cakes and order properly, instead of me chasing quotes on WhatsApp.” Storefront + branding + order/quote management + the A4 simulator. Saved templates 3 → 30 is a secondary nudge, not the headline.

Flame → Blaze ★: premium storefront template (#6, customer-facing — strongest) · full X-Ray reports (#16) · bigger smart-tools allowance (#13) · storefront analytics (#7, v1.1). This is where the domain-specific production tools live, and it is the step with the most headroom to price into.

Blaze → Forge: ⚠️ EMPTY AT MVP. Was seats + priority support + (later) granular staff control; with staff logins deferred (2026-07-27) only priority support remains, and a support policy is not a tier. Open question — decide before correcting the marketing page: (1) ship two paid tiers and bring Forge back with seats, (2) keep Forge as a SERVICE tier — “Studio · from ₹4,999 · talk to us”, sold on support/onboarding rather than features (current lean: honest, and preserves the anchor), or (3) build something specifically to justify it — wrong order to do things in. Whichever wins, its price stays visible: “call our sales team” was considered and rejected — hiding price loses 70–80% of buyers, the contact-sales pattern only pays above ~$25k ACV (Forge is ~$600), and a decoy with no number anchors nothing.

Hiding a plan — use the flag that already exists

Forge is already in the system, so “don’t launch Forge yet” is a DATA change, not a code change: update subscription_plans set is_active = false where name = 'forge';

What it hides: GET /api/plans filters is_active, and that ONE endpoint feeds the marketing catalog, the billing plan picker and the signup wizard. Flip the flag, Forge vanishes from all three, no deploy.

What it does NOT touch (verified by tracing every reader of the table): existing Forge subscribers keep working — entitlements resolve by plan_id with no is_active filter, so hiding a plan ≠ deactivating a customer. Admin still sees and can edit it (/admin/subscription-plans selects *). Nothing else reads the flag.

⚠️ Known gap: POST /billing/subscribe resolves the tier through the PLAN.ID_BY_NAME code constant, not the table, so it does not check is_active. A direct API call with tier:'forge' would still be accepted. Fine for MVP (unreachable through the product), but the route must validate the plan is active before this is treated as a real gate rather than a display toggle.

Do not add a new column. is_active already means “publicly listed and subscribable”, and for Forge at MVP those are the same thing. A separate is_listed only earns its keep if we ever need listed-but-not-subscribable, which we do not.

Market — who we are selling to

~5 lakh active home bakers in India, plus ~10 lakh unorganised bakeries (only ~2,000 organised). Bakery market USD 15.05B → 32.05B by 2034.

40–60% of custom cake orders already come via Instagram / WhatsApp — Flame’s pitch is not a hypothesis, it describes what half the market does today.

Segment split below is an ESTIMATE, not data — no source segments the 5 lakh by revenue. Hypothesis to validate against our own signups: hobby <10 orders/mo ~325k (trial only) · part-time 10–30 ~110k (Flame) · full-time 30–60 ~50k (Blaze) · studio 60+ ~15k (Forge). At 2% penetration ≈ ₹62L/month, ~₹7.4Cr ARR.

Pricing model — decided, do not reopen

Feature tiers + metered smart tools. No order cap, ever. “Features vs order caps” is a false binary: usage-based SaaS runs 115–130% NRR vs 95–105% flat-rate, but the usage lever does not have to be ORDERS. Ours is smart tools (#13). Order volume is customer-driven — the wrong actor to cap.

Metered tease, not preview. A capped allowance of a premium feature beats hiding it AND beats a preview: a preview shows what you’re missing, five real build guides make you feel what you’d lose. X-Ray meters confirmed orders (first 5 per calendar month), never report views — punishing a baker for re-opening the guide mid-bake would break the one moment it matters. Applies to #13, #16, #21 — never to category features.

PQL beats the calendar. Product-qualified prompting converts at 25–30% vs 5–10% for time-triggered. Our PQL = storefront published AND ≥1 quote request received. Highest-leverage change available, and it needs no pricing change at all.

Tiers: Flame (entry) · Blaze ★ (the target — serious home baker) · Forge (studio / multi-staff). Spark = 30-day trial, not a tier. Deferred & unmarketed: #8 white-label, #9 custom domain, #20 WhatsApp. Killed: element-tier-gating, order caps on paid tiers.

Prices held at ₹999 / ₹2,499 / ₹4,999 (2026-07-26). Benchmark against vertical SaaS sold to Indian SMBs (Petpooja ₹3,000–12,000/mo), not horizontal store builders — those set what a baker thinks software costs, not what this is worth. A full-time home baker makes 30–60 cakes/mo at ₹1,400–2,400 and 60–70% margin, so one extra order a month covers Flame or Blaze. Full derivation, rejected options and sources in SUBSCRIPTION_TIERS.md.
